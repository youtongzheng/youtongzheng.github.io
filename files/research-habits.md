# WEAVE Lab research habits

Source: https://youtongzheng.github.io/handbook/#research-habits (Youtong Zheng's group, University of Houston).

These habits apply to day-to-day research in the group, especially in a student's first years.

## The habits

### 1. Mistakes

- In the first one or two years, mistakes are expected, and Youtong will help fix them. Repeating the same basic mistake is not acceptable.
- Units: a number with the wrong unit is meaningless. Check the units of every variable first whenever you look at a plot.
- Leaving things to the last minute (candidacy exam, annual review, abstract submissions, papers): rushing at the end goes wrong, and the cost falls on Youtong, the committee, and collaborators. After it happens once or twice, plan further ahead.
- Reflect: after something goes wrong, ask why it happened and what you'll do differently.

### 2. Understand a new dataset or tool before using it

- Learn its uncertainty and limitations first.
- Make basic plots that show what the data looks like, and run sanity checks (for example, values in a physically reasonable range).
- Plot the intermediate steps, not just the final figure, so a strange result can be traced to real physics or a bug.

### 3. Independence grows over time

- Youtong is hands-on in the first one or two years.
- After that, become more independent step by step: learn new things on your own and bring your own ideas.
- By the fourth or fifth year, lead your own project.

### 4. Take initiative

- Don't wait to be pushed. Think through the next step on your own.
- When stuck and getting nowhere, come to Youtong for help. Youtong's job is to help, not to chase.

### 5. Share results as a story deck, not a long email

- Work through the logic first, build a short deck with a clear story (see the one-on-one meeting rules), send it, then meet.

### 6. Working in the lab

- Not required; progress is judged by what you produce.
- In Youtong's experience, the students seen most often in the lab are usually the most productive.
- If progress stalls, Youtong will ask you to work in the lab.

### 7. Code and data

- Formal work must be: a paper, a formal presentation, or an important unpublished result worth revisiting. Keep the scripts, the data and its source, and the settings so it can be rerun months later with the same result.

## Instructions for an AI agent helping a student

1. Before analyzing a new dataset, ask for or produce basic characterization plots and sanity checks, including units.
2. When a result looks strange, check the intermediate steps before interpreting it.
3. For formal work (papers, formal presentations, important results), make sure the figures come from scripts that can be rerun, and record the data source.
4. When the student has a deadline (exam, review, abstract, paper), help them make a plan with time to spare.
5. When the student wants to report results, suggest a short story deck rather than a long email.
