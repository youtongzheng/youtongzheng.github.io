Research

We weave observations, simulations, theory, and machine learning together to understand clouds, convection, and precipitation.

Coastal storms and kilometer-scale models

Why Houston's thunderstorms form where they do, and whether air pollution makes them stronger.

Sea-breeze thunderstorms bring much of the Texas Gulf Coast's rain and flooding. We study how they start, move inland, and respond to the city and its air pollution. A main tool is the U.S. Department of Energy's storm-resolving model (E3SM/SCREAM), which we test against observations from the DOE TRACER field campaign in Houston, radar, and satellites. This work is funded by a DOE Early Career Award.

Shallow clouds, aerosols, and climate

How low clouds over oceans and the Arctic form, break up, and respond to warming and aerosols.

Low clouds cool the planet, and how they change with warming is one of the largest uncertainties in climate projections. We use large-eddy simulations, global models, and field and satellite observations to study how stratocumulus decks break up into cumulus, how rain evaporates below clouds, how aerosols change cloud water, and how Arctic clouds respond to sea-ice loss.

Machine learning that reasons physically

Models, including AI, should get the right answer for the right physical reason.

We build machine-learning models that say how much they can be trusted and that can be read in physical terms: when a model predicts that a storm will start, we want to know when that prediction is reliable and why. The group also tests AI agents for research, such as agents that run and check climate-model analyses, with the rule that numbers come from trusted, verified code.

Cloud-base updrafts from satellites

Retrieving the vertical air motion that feeds clouds, from space.

The updrafts at cloud base control how many droplets a cloud forms. Youtong's PhD and postdoctoral work developed theory and satellite methods to estimate these updrafts and cloud-top radiative cooling, and used them to show how aerosols change ocean clouds.
