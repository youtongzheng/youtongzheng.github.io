Teaching & Mentoring

Students understand the atmosphere when they reason their way to it, and the most valuable skill a scientist can have is the judgment to tell whether a result deserves trust. Those two ideas shape how Youtong teaches and mentors.

Approach · Courses · What students say · Mentoring

Approach

One question at a time

Each lecture is a chain of questions. Each slide answers one and raises the next, and at every turning point the question goes to the class. In Synoptic Meteorology, students build vertical motion link by link, from the wind field to the ageostrophic wind to divergence, and then use the chain on a 300-hPa map to work out why the weather is fair ahead of a ridge and wet ahead of a trough. By the time the equation appears, students want it.

Concept, tool, decision

Meteorology is taught as a chain: understand the physics, learn the tool that measures or predicts it, then use both to make a forecast or a decision. In large classes, live polls (Top Hat, Mentimeter), short forecasting exercises, practice quizzes, and short videos keep everyone thinking.

Learning from students

When a student wrote that math first and physics later made it "hard to connect the dots," every equation was rebuilt to arrive as the answer to a physical question. Reanalysis case studies of cyclones, map-based group exercises, and frequent low-stakes practice followed.

Courses at the University of Houston

GEOL 1347

Introduction to Meteorology (with labs)

General education, all majors

High- and low-pressure systems, air masses, clouds, the jet stream, precipitation, air–sea interactions, local and global circulations, and how to read a weather map. One of the largest classes at UH, with up to 424 students in a section.

GEOL 4349/6397 New course developed

Cloud Physics

Undergraduate and graduate

How clouds form, how droplets and ice grow into rain and snow, and how clouds shape weather and climate, from the cloud droplet to the cloud field.

Taught (enrollment): Spring 2024 (8)Spring 2025 (16)

GEOL 4352/6397 New course developed

Synoptic Meteorology (with lab)

Undergraduate and graduate · core course of the B.S. in Atmospheric Science

The processes behind winds, temperature change, and precipitation; how mid-latitude cyclones and fronts develop and decay, and their hazards. Built from scratch for UH's new B.S. in Atmospheric Science, and designed to meet the federal GS-1340 meteorologist requirement and the AMS guidelines for a bachelor's degree in atmospheric science.

Mentoring

Two principles guide mentoring in the group. Students grow by owning a real question, with more guidance at the start and less as they learn to diagnose their own results. And students should be trained for the field they will enter: since 2026, a weekly AI Agent Day teaches everyone to use AI agents in research, and to check every AI result against something the AI did not make, such as observations, a reference simulation, a budget that must close, or a number worked out by hand.
